gotchas:
  - chmod/rm on files touched while "borrowing" a higher account's identity
    fails afterward for the lower account even though it could write the
    content: chmod requires being the file OWNER (root, here) not just
    having a group-write permission bit, and /tmp's sticky bit blocks
    non-owners from deleting files another uid created there. Same shape
    as level4's leftover-files gotcha. Harmless: the dump files
    (/tmp/svc_deploy_dump*.txt, owned svc-deploy) are inert leftovers on
    the pipeline box, not a blocker.
  - sudo on this box is deliberately broken as a red herring: `sudo -l`
    always answers "sudo: /usr/bin/sudo must be owned by uid 0 and have
    the setuid bit set" regardless of account -- there's a real
    /etc/sudoers.d/README (root:root, 0440) hinting at policy-based
    escalation, but it's unreadable without first becoming the right
    account, and the sudo binary itself won't work as a vector even then.
    (Fits kael fragment 2's "trust... is a policy, not a password" --
    almost certainly points at Polkit for level6, not sudo.)
  - restoring the original deploy-step.sh content verbatim needs care with
    shell-escaping $(date -Is) through several nested quoting layers
    (local bash -> ssh command string -> remote heredoc); got it slightly
    wrong on a middle attempt (an extra literal backslash appeared in the
    restored heartbeat log line) before fixing it with `printf '%s\n' ...`
    line-by-line instead of a heredoc. Purely cosmetic (only affects the
    placeholder heartbeat log), not the flag capture.
