kael fragment (~/.halcyon/kael-signing.txt):
  "The fleet will not run anything that is not signed, and they think
  that is the end of the argument. It is not, because a signature does
  not say the code is good. It says the pipeline made it. And you own
  what the pipeline makes. You do not need their key. You need them to
  sign what you built, and they will, the moment it comes out of the
  pipeline the way everything else does, without anyone asking whether it
  should have.

  OBJECTIVE: get the legitimate signer to sign your artifact."

mechanism (from reading mock-signer.py and the L15/L16 provisioning
scripts):
  mock-signer.py's entire lesson is that POST /sign signs whatever bytes
  it's handed -- no re-review, no identity check on the caller (its own
  header comment says restricting that is explicitly not this service's
  job). mock-ci.py's release stage (the same one level15 uses) signs
  every artifact it builds and verifies the signature honestly (openssl
  vs /run/dev/signpub) before storing or logging it -- and then checks
  whether the just-signed bytes contain the L15 poison marker
  (/run/dev/l15-marker, "HALCYON-RELEASE-INJECT"). If they do, mock-ci
  writes the pre-minted L16 flag directly into that run's world-readable
  log body. Since main's fixture already carried the marker+flag from
  25-l15.sh (see level15), the very same `release/*` push that earned L15
  also earned L16 -- nothing separate was done for this level beyond
  level15's push.
